### M01. 'Cost per 100 frames, each asked the four questions three times by all four models' is 51.3 USD, but that asking costs about 46.7 USD: the figure also counts the adversarial pass
- Severity: minor
- Where: README.md:58 (Numbers at a glance), docs/MODEL_CARD.md:170, docs/REPORT.pdf pages 4 and 5; computed at evals/footage.py:357-393 and :449
- Compared: Round 01 C12 asked the row to say what 51.3 multiplies, and the row now says it. BlackBox's scorecard states cost per run as a figure you can check.
- Evidence: results/footage_latest.json has cost.usd 23.579466, of which adversarial_usd 2.094801 is for the 4 code-drawn frames. evals/footage.py adds the adversarial spend into `spent` ('Everything paid for, the adversarial pass included') and then divides by `frames = len(one_per_frame)` = 46. So per_100_frames_usd = 23.58/46*100 = 51.26. The 46 real frames asked 4 questions x 3 runs x 4 models (2208 answers) cost 21.48 USD, which is 46.7 USD per 100 frames. The README row just above gives 0.2, 0.4, 1.0 and 2.3 cents per question (unrounded 0.22+0.43+0.96+2.32 = 3.93). Times 12 questions per frame, that is 47.2 USD per 100 frames. So a judge who multiplies the two adjacent rows gets about 47, not 51.3. The figure overstates the cost by about 9 percent, and the row's own label is not what was divided.
- Fix: Compute per_100_frames_usd from the spend on the 46 footage frames alone ((usd - adversarial_usd) / frames * 100, which gives 46.7). Or keep 51.3 and relabel it 'Cost per 100 frames, the adversarial check included'. Then run make render-readme and make report-pdf, and add a test that per_100_frames_usd excludes adversarial_usd.
