## River Habitat Survey (channel and bank cues)

- RHS manual 2003, 2022 reprint (Environment Agency, LIT 1758), PDF: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/1093961/RHS-manual-2003_2022-reprint-LIT-1758.pdf . Used: spot-check key page 14, "Channel modification indicators: uniform bank profile; straightened planform; bankfull width/bankfull height ratio <4:1; uniform/low energy flow-types; no trees/uniformly-aged trees along bank; intensive/urban land-use"; Section E page 34, "Resectioned (reprofiled) bank (RS)": re-profiling "will produce a relatively smooth, uniformly angled, bank slope"; "Reinforced bank (RI)": "concrete, sheet piling, corrugated iron, wood piling, gabion, brick/laid stone, rip-rap"; Section D "Is channel obviously realigned?": "re-aligned channels are typically straight"; page 3 paragraph 1.8, "Surveyor accreditation is needed for data to be entered onto the RHS database. This means surveyors attending a training course using the 2003 version, and passing an accreditation test"; page 20, "Only surveys from accredited surveyors will be entered on the RHS database"; Section C riffle and pool definitions; Section E flow types (smooth, no perceptible flow, unbroken and broken standing waves, chute, free fall).

## Dry-weather outfall screening (the pipe rule)

- EPA and Center for Watershed Protection, "Illicit Discharge Detection and Elimination: A Guidance Manual for Program Development and Technical Assessments" (2004), PDF: https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-11/documents/idde_manualwithappendices.pdf . Page 337 (chapter 12): "the traditional definition of dry weather has been 72 hours without rainfall, some communities have shortened this window to 48 hours". Page 6 (chapter 1): "Not all dry weather storm drain flow contains pollutants or pathogens ... Flow in these drains may be derived from springs, groundwater seepage, or leaks from water distribution pipes. Consequently, field testing and/or water quality sampling are needed to confirm whether pollutants are actually present in dry weather flow". Page 5: an illicit discharge is "a storm drain that has measurable flow during dry weather containing pollutants and/or pathogens". Chapter 11: sensory indicators at flowing outfalls are odor, color, turbidity and floatables; physical indicators at any outfall include deposits or stains and abnormal vegetation.
- EPA Stormwater Phase II factsheet 2.5, Illicit Discharge Detection and Elimination (2018), PDF: https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2018-12/documents/epa_stormwater_phase_ii_final_rule_factsheet_2.5_illicit_discharge_12-04-18.pdf . Used for the regulatory definition of an illicit discharge.

## Safe practice for people and pets

- CDC, Preventing Illness from Harmful Algal Blooms: https://www.cdc.gov/harmful-algal-blooms/prevention/index.html . "If water looks or smells bad, stay out and keep kids, pets, and livestock away"; signs are water that "smells bad, looks discolored, has foam, scum, mats of algae, or paint-like streaks on the surface"; "If you do go in or touch water that may have a harmful algal bloom, rinse off immediately after"; "If your animals do go in water ... rinse them off immediately after. Do not let them lick their fur before you rinse them"; "If your pets or livestock seem sick after going in or near water, call a veterinarian right away".
- CDC, Healthy Swimming, Steps to Take: https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/prevention/index.html . "Don't swallow the water"; "Protect open cuts or wounds"; "Stay out if you see pipes. Pipes draining into or around the water could be putting germs or harmful chemicals into the water"; "Wash your hands before eating". Also fetched: https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/about/index.html (overview only).
- California State Water Resources Control Board, freshwater and estuarine harmful algal blooms: https://www.waterboards.ca.gov/water_issues/programs/swamp/freshwater_cyanobacteria.html (first reported dog illness at Clear Lake, 1925) and the California HABs portal https://mywaterquality.ca.gov/habs/ . Fetched for context; the sentences cite the CDC pages above.
